The City of Los Angeles has the largest street network in the nation with over 7,500 miles of roads. The Bureau of Street Services (referred to as BSS, and branded as StreetsLA), is the agency responsible for maintaining this sprawling network.

Unfortunately, we are standing at the edge of a financial cliff. Despite LA’s heavy car traffic our streets receive less investment than peer cities (for example, about a third of what Long Beach spends per mile of road). Decades of underinvestment in accessible curb ramps combined with a growing Citywide budget crisis means that the status quo is unsustainable.

By modeling road degradation rates we are able to estimate how different budget and policy scenarios lead to a range of outcomes. Pavement Condition Index (PCI) is a 0-100 score measuring road quality. 

**LA’s current average PCI of 71 is on track to fall to 41 by 2035. At that point a super majority of streets in the City will be in a failed state and cumulatively will cost over $15.5 billion to fix.**

<b  class="highlights">
1) LA has a $8B street and access ramp maintenance backlog. We are on track for that growing to over $15B by 2035.
3) Large asphalt repair isn't working. It produces no meaningful PCI gain and $1B in additional deferred maintenance over the next decade.
2) To maintain a state of good repair BSS requires a budget of $350M/year. The City has rarely ever budgeted more than $100M.
4) Access ramp costs are an outsized burden. At the current price of $50k per ramp it will cost $4 Billion to fix all ramps in the City. At the price of peer cities (for example, $10k in Beverly Hills) fixing all access ramps would cost less than $900m.
